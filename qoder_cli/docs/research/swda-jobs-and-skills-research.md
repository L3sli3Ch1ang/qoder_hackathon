# SWDA Jobs-Skills Portal Research

> Research record of the **SkillsFuture / Skills & Workforce Development Agency (SWDA) "Jobs-Skills Portal"** — covering Insights, Skills Frameworks, and Data & Tools.
>
> **Source:** https://jobsandskills.swda.gov.sg
> **Retrieved:** 29 July 2026 (portal content last updated 30–31 July 2026)
> **Method:** Live browser research (the portal returns HTTP 403 to simple fetchers, so a real browser session was used to render and extract content, including sub-tabs and download links).

A collaboration between the **Skills & Workforce Development Agency (SWDA)** and **GovTech**. Described as "a one-stop platform for all your Jobs-Skills data needs."

---

## Executive Summary

The portal is Singapore's official one-stop platform for jobs-and-skills labour-market intelligence. Three pillars were researched:

1. **Insights** — headline publication is the annual **Skills Demand for the Future Economy (SDFE) Report** (latest edition: **SDFE 2025**), plus bite-size **Jobs-Skills Insights (SFJSI)**. The SDFE 2025 report focuses on the **Care, Digital, and Green economies**.
2. **Skills Frameworks (SFw)** — government-developed frameworks (with employers, industry, unions, professional bodies) outlining key skills, job roles, and training across **38 sectors**. Three **quarterly downloadable datasets** are published.
3. **Data and Tools** — a suite of **10 public dashboards** and **4 partner-exclusive "Pro" tools** (including the **Integrated Insights Tool**), all built on SWDA datasets and algorithms.

**Direct relevance to SkillBridge:** the portal's 38-sector Skills Frameworks, the downloadable Skills Framework Dataset (job roles + key tasks + skills), the List of Skills, and the Priority Skills (Care/Digital/Green) analysis are authoritative, machine-readable sources that could ground SkillBridge's skill taxonomy, sector list, and course mapping in real Singapore workforce data.

### 1.1 SDFE 2025 Report (latest edition)

**Full report download (PDF):** `https://jobsandskills.swda.gov.sg/sdfe-2025-published/assets/files/SSG_2024_12.pdf`

> "The SDFE 2025 report provides an essential, updated insight into Singapore's evolving job market, focusing on the **Care, Digital, and Green economies**."

The report "revolutionizes workforce intelligence through its sophisticated mapping of skills, technology, and career pathways," helping businesses anticipate talent needs and individuals chart career transitions. Its multilayered methodology analyses emerging technologies and strategic industries.

**2025 edition enhancements / new dashboards:**
- Linking Skills and Functions at Work
- Skills Matrix
- Enhanced Identification of Pathways Leading to In-demand Jobs
- Creative Industries

**Report chapters (sub-pages):**

| # | Chapter | URL | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Job Requirements (Changes in Requirements of Job Roles) | `/sdfe-2025/introduction` | How skill requirements and use of digital Apps & Tools at work have changed (2019–2023 job-posting data). Skill clusters simplified 11 → 6. Includes AI-related Apps & Tools demand. |
| 2 | Priority Skills Forecast and Movement | `/sdfe-2025/priority-skills` | Movement trends of Priority Skills in the Care, Digital, and Green economies (2022–2024). Four-quadrant Skills Matrix. 12-month demand forecast. Eleven skills to watch. |

#### Chapter 1 — Job Requirements (key points)
- Uses **five years of job-posting data (2019–2023)** to show how skill requirements and workplace digital **Apps & Tools** have changed.
- Skill clusters **simplified from 11 to 6** to align with broad business functions and Critical Core Skills.
- A comprehensive curated **list of Apps & Tools** is provided.
- Sub-analyses: Skill clusters of the SG economy; Demand of Apps & Tools by work function (2023); Top 20 Apps & Tools (2023 + historical rankings); **Demand of AI-related Apps & Tools** by work function (2023); Top 20 AI-related Apps & Tools.
- Accompanied by the **Job Requirements Dashboard**.

#### Chapter 2 — Priority Skills (key points)
- Analyses **Priority Skills for the Care, Digital, and Green economies** using **2022–2024** historical data.
- **Four-quadrant Skills Matrix** classifies each skill-year by demand × transferability: **In-demand / Up-and-coming / Niche / Transferable**.
- **Forecasts demand of Priority Skills for the next 12 months**; highlights **eleven skills to watch** across the three economies.
- Observations: skills consistently In-demand; skills moving from moderate → high transferability; skills forecast to become highly transferable in 2025.
- Special mentions: Essential Skills of HR Professionals (Care); **AI Ethics and Governance** (Digital); **AI Application and ESG Reporting** (Digital); **Building Sustainability Skills in Engineering Professionals** (Green); Understanding Skills Movements for career self-management.
- Accompanied by the **Priority Skills Dashboard**.

#### Chapter 3 — Creative Industries (key points)
- Examples: Changi Airport's "Wonderfall" (3D modelling/simulation/graphics); NLB's StoryGen (GenAI → immersive multimedia).
- Studies: skills/tools for creative professionals; **growing demand for creative skills in non-creative sectors**; **impact of GenAI** on creative work.
- **~150,000 creative professionals** in Singapore; only ~1/3 work directly in a creative sector. Singapore ranked **5th in Global Innovation**, partly contributed by Creative Industries.

#### Chapter 4 — Reskilling Pathways (key points)
- Five factors for evaluating career opportunities: **Wage**, **Demand for jobs**, **Growth of job roles** (3-year trend), **Skills similarity** (overlap/transferability), **Historical transitions** across job roles. Plus VIPS (Values, Interest, Personality, Skills) and practical considerations.
- Incorporated into the **Skills and Job Mobility Dashboard** (on the Jobs-Skills Portal) to explore career pathways.

### 1.2 Jobs-Skills Insights (SFJSI)
- Bite-size, frequently updated insights on fast-paced jobs-and-skills changes across sectors and economies. Latest: `/insights/sfjsi/latest-insights`.

## 2. Skills Frameworks (`/frameworks/skills-frameworks`)

> "Explore job roles and essential skills aligned with industry needs. Developed in partnership with employers, industry leaders, education institutions, unions and government for the workforce."

### 2.1 What Skills Frameworks (SFw) are
- "Skills Frameworks outline **key skills, job roles, and training** to build specialised expertise, enhance business performance and support employment and employability."
- Developed for the Singapore workforce by the government **in partnership with employers, industry associations, unions, and professional bodies**.
- SWDA keeps them current via a **continuous skills updating and discovery process** using dynamic labour-market intelligence and expert validation.

### 2.2 Interactive Skills Framework (online tool)
- URL: `/frameworks/interactive-skills-frameworks`
- Search, explore, and understand skills information **without spreadsheets**.
- Key features: explore skills **by job role**, search by **Skill Title**, or **browse by sector**; view skills using **different skills languages** for different use cases.

### 2.3 Downloadable Skills Framework datasets (updated quarterly)
See the [Downloadable data](#4-downloadable-data-and-tools) section for the direct links. Three files:
1. **Unique Skills List** — updated list of unique skills in the latest **Skills Framework 2.0 language**; expert-validated. Used for SSG-funded course-to-skills tagging; referenced by SWDA's **Course Approval Skills List (CASL)** and **Emerging Skills** List.
2. **Skills Framework Dataset** — comprehensive data on **job roles, key tasks, and skills across 38 sectors**; key resource for WSQ course accreditation (work tasks, proficiency levels, knowledge & abilities).
3. **TSC to Unique Skills Mapping File** — maps sectoral Skills Framework skills to their equivalent **Unique Skill(s)**; updated quarterly to track changes.

### 2.4 Skills Frameworks by Sector (38 sectors)
From `/frameworks/sector-information` — "Explore the skills frameworks for **38 sectors**, outlining key trends, roles, and capabilities to guide workforce development." Each sector provides resources for workforce planning, curriculum design, and career development.

## 3. Data and Tools (`/data-and-tools`)

> "This section houses Jobs-Skills Data and Tools where users can self-serve to gather insights into the interactions between jobs and skills, and their demand and supply in Singapore's labour market."

Two categories: **Dashboards** (public) and **Tools** (partner-exclusive Pro tools).

### 3.1 Integrated Insights Tool (the requested page)
URL: `/data-and-tools/tools/integrated-insights-tool`

> "This tool brings together labour-market insights on **jobs, skills, and course availability** to support programme planning and development. Use it to identify in-demand job roles and skills, explore **CASL and Emerging Skills**, and assess available training options."

- **Access:** exclusive to **registered users** (login required). Not publicly viewable without an account.
- **Audience:** curriculum developers, training providers, workforce stakeholders.
- **Structure (3 tabs):**
  - **Jobs Tab** — Explore Job Role Demand; understand displayed info; explore Job Role Details; identify in-demand skills across multiple job roles; **download Job Role Insights**.
  - **Skills Tab** — Explore Skills Demand; explore Skill Details; identify job roles across multiple skills; **download Skills Insights**.
  - **Available Courses Tab** — Explore available courses by skill; find courses linked to **CASL and Emerging Skills**.
- **Guided walkthrough:** 45-minute self-paced e-learning by the Centre for Skills-First Practices, **Institute for Adult Learning (IAL)** — https://www.ial.edu.sg/programmes/skills-first-capability-development-programmes/
- **Methodology:** uses datasets and algorithms developed by SWDA — `/data-and-tools/methodology/tools-methodology`.

## 5. Notes and Caveats

- **Report-year lag:** The user asked to constrain to the "latest report in the current year (2026)." The latest SDFE edition is **SDFE 2025** (there is not yet a 2026 edition). The portal itself was last updated **30–31 July 2026**, and the quarterly datasets are current to **Q2/Q3 2026** (Unique Skills List Jun 2026; Skills Framework Dataset Q3 2026). Underlying SDFE data vintages: job-posting data 2019–2023 (Ch. 1) and 2022–2024 (Ch. 2).
- **Access restrictions:** The static `WebFetch` tool receives **HTTP 403** from this portal (bot protection); a real browser session was required. The **Integrated Insights Tool** and the other **Pro tools** are **registration/partner-gated** — their inner content was not accessed (no credentials used). Descriptions are from the public landing pages.
- **Download links** are `file.go.gov.sg` short links as published on the portal; they redirect to the hosted `.xlsx` files.
- **Sector counts:** Skills Frameworks cover **38 sectors**; the SDFE 2025 report's analytical focus is the **Care, Digital, and Green economies** (cross-cutting themes rather than the 38 administrative sectors).
- All URLs are relative to `https://jobsandskills.swda.gov.sg` unless shown in full.

## 6. Relevance to SkillBridge (hackathon context)

- **Authoritative skill taxonomy:** the **Skills Framework Dataset** (job roles + key tasks + skills across 38 sectors) and the **Unique Skills List** (SF 2.0 language) are machine-readable `.xlsx` sources that could replace/augment SkillBridge's hand-built `skill_taxonomy.json`.
- **Sector alignment:** SkillBridge's 5 sectors map cleanly onto SWDA's 38 (see §2.4 table) — useful for validating the demo's sector names and for a "real data" story to judges.
- **Priority Skills & transferability:** the SDFE four-quadrant Skills Matrix (In-demand / Up-and-coming / Niche / Transferable) and the "skills similarity" concept in Ch. 4 mirror SkillBridge's own matching/surprise-filter logic — strong narrative alignment.
- **Course mapping:** the Available Courses tab and CASL/Emerging Skills lists correspond to SkillBridge's course-mapper stage.
- **Credibility/evidence:** citing the official SWDA/GovTech portal and its datasets strengthens the "real-world grounding" of the submission.
